SYMPLOCACEAE
H.P. Nooteboom
Netherlands Centre for Biodiversity Naturalis (section NHN), Leiden University, Leiden, The Netherlands
Desf., Mem. Mus. Hist. Nat. Paris 6 (1820) 9 (as Symploceae); G. Don, Gen. Syst. 4 (1837) 1; Brand, Pflanzenr. 6 (1901) 1; Hutchinson, Fam. Flow. PL, 2nd ed. 1 (1959) 170; Nooteboom, Leiden Bot. Ser. 1 (1975) 33, FL Malesiana 1, 8 (1977) 205, Fam. Gen. Vase. PL 6 (2004) 443; Culham in Heywood et al., Flow. PL Fam. World (2007) 314; Mabberley, PL Book, 3rd ed. (2008) 834.
A monogeneric family.
Taxonomy. Symplocaceae is placed in the Ericales and is most closely related to the Styracaceae (Stevens, AP website, 22 June 2009).
SYMPLOCOS Jacq. (Greek, sym- = together, plocos = connected; referring to the stamens adnate to the corolla)
Sawa lukah (Malay)
Enum. PL Carib. (1760) 5, 24; Linnaeus, Gen. PL ed. 6 (1764) 272; Bentham & Hooker f., Gen. Pl. 2 (1876) 668; C.B. Clarke in Hooker f., FL Brit. India 3 (1882) 572; King & Gamble, J. As. Soc. Beng. 74, 2, (1906) 231; Brand, Pflanzenr. 6 (1901) 13; Ridley, FL Malay Pen. 2 (1923) 298; Backer& Bakhuizen f, FL Java2 (1965) 204; Nooteboom, Leiden Bot. Ser. 1 (1975) 33, FL Malesiana 1, 8 (1977) 205, Fam. Gen. Vase. PL 6 (2004) 448, Blumea 50 (2005) 407; Kochummen, Tr. FL Malaya 3 (1978) 266; Pearce, Tr. FL Sabah & Sarawak 5 (2004) 389. Synonyms: Cordyloblaste Moritzi, Bot. Zeit. 6 (1848) 606, Ridley, FL Malay Pen. 2 (1923) 307; Symplocos Jacq. sect. Cordyloblaste (Moritzi) Benth. & Hookf, Gen. PL 2 (1876) 669, Brand, Pflanzenr. 6 (1901) 26, 88; van Steenis, Bull. Bot. Gard. Buitenz. 3, 17 (1948) 429.
Small, evergreen (exceptionally deciduous) shrubs to (rarely) huge trees. Growth continuous, leaves evenly distributed (or rarely intermittent and the buds are often protected by leathery scales). Indumentum of simple hairs may be present on all the parts of the plant, at least in young stages. Leaves simple, spirally or distichously arranged, rarely pseudo-verticillate, exstipulate; petiole always present, rarely very short; laminas penninerved, in many species drying yellowish green; midrib impressed above but in Symplocos theaefolia and S. anomala midrib flat or prominent above. Inflorescence a spike, raceme or panicle, sometimes condensed to dense clusters, from the upper leaf axils or beneath them from the axils of fallen leaves, rarely flowers solitary; flowers subtended by a bract (exceptionally bractless in the axil of a leaf) and 2 bracteoles, rarely several bracts and bracteoles by abortion of flowers. Flowers actinomorphic, bisexual, rarely by reduction unisexual and the plant androdioecious (e.g., S. wikstroemifolia), fragrant; calyx lobes (3–)5, imbricate, persistent, sometimes split into two parts and seemingly 2-lobed; in subgen. Symplocos corolla glabrous but the lobes

@page 9 220
sometimes ciliolate, (except for S. odoratissima with a hairy corolla) divided nearly to the base, connate in a tube in subgen. Cordyloblaste (S. henschelii and S. pendula), the lobes (3–)5, quincuncially imbricate, usually white or yellowish or greenish; stamens (4–) mostly many (–150 in S. nivea), joined in a long monadelphous tube in subgen. Cordyloblaste, but in subgen. Symplocos only joined at the very base, monadelphous or pentadelphous and then the bundles alternipetalous; staminal tube always inserted on the base of the corolla; anthers globose, bilocular, introrse, dehiscing longitudinally; intrastaminal disc glandular, sometimes inconspicuous and then not mentioned in the description; pistil 1, ovary inferior (to slightly semi-inferior), (2–)3–5-locular, septation complete, style 1, glabrous or hairy and then mentioned in the description, stigma punctiform or peltate, ovules 2–4 in each locule. Fruit a drupe with one pyrene (stone), glabrous (rarely hairy), often crowned by the persistent calyx lobes rarely the lobes caducous, leaving a rim, globose, flask-shaped, ellipsoid, ovoid, obovoid, spindle-shaped or cylindric, mesocarp usually thin, but sometimes thick and then often quite hard, when ripe frequently bluish; stone rarely smooth, often ribbed lengthwise (or with irregular ridges or grooves), in cross-section sometimes star-shaped when the ribs are very high. Seeds straight or curved, 1 in each developed locule (the number of locules is often not assessed as the stones mostly are aluminium encrusted, and very hard) ; endosperm copious; embryo straight or curved (in cases where the embryo is once or twice curved usually only one cell develops), cotyledons very short, linear.
Distribution. About 260 species. Eastern parts of the Old World, from India (Mumbai) to Fiji, and from 46° N in northern China to Australia (New South Wales) and Lord Howe Island at 32° S, and in the New World from North America (Washington State) to South America (southern Brazil). In Peninsular Malaysia, 21 species of which 3 (Symplocos calycactylos, S. nivea and S. pyriflora) are endemic. Symplocos theifolia, recorded in Singapore, is also included in this account.
Ecology. In temperate and tropical regions, in the tropics most abundant in mountains, to c. 4000 m on G. Kinabalu and the New Guinea mountains, where it is represented by microphyllous dwarf shrubs. Everywhere a constituent of mixed, mostly evergreen rain forest, not in arid zones.
Uses. Some species of Symplocos accumulate aluminium and their bark and leaves have been used as a yellow dye for batik cloth or woven pandan and rattan or as a mordant for the red dye from Morinda but in Peninsular Malaysia chemical dyes are now used. Aluminium accumulation is associated with leaves that dry yellow-green and with blue fruits that are found in this genus (Mabberley, 2008).
Taxonomy. Ridley (1923) following Moritzi (1848) recognised Cordyloblaste as a separate genus. It is here treated as a subgenus with two species (Symplocos henschelii and S. pendula), the remaining 20 species fall within subgen. Symplocos. Due to the occurrence of species complexes, sometimes subspecies and varieties are recognised. For full synonymy of the subspecies and varieties oftaxa that occur outside Peninsular Malaysia see Nooteboom (1975, 1977 & http://www.nationaalherbarium.nl/Symplocosweb/Symplocos.html).
