PENTAPHYLACACEAE
T.L. Yao
Forest Research Institute Malaysia, Kepong, Malaysia
Engler, Nat. Pflanzenfam., Nachtr. 1 (1897) 214 s.s., ibid. 3 (1908) 197; Steenis, Fl. Malesiana 1, 5 (1955) 121; Hutchinson, Fam. Flow. Pl. 1, 2nd ed. (1959) 276; Whitmore, Tr. Fl. Malaya 2 (1973) 308.
Monogeneric family.
Taxonomy. Gardner & Champion (J. Bot. & Kew Gard. Misc. 1 (1849) 244) placed Pentaphylax in the Ternstroemiaceae, as did Ridley (Fl. Malay Pen. 1 (1922) 205). However, Engler (1897) described Pentaphylacaceae as a family distinct from Ternstroemiaceae based on its spirally arranged leaves and five anthers with terminal pores opening with small lids, compared with Ternstroemiaceae with alternate leaves and numerous anthers in several series, opening lengthwise and only very rarely having terminal pores. Van Steenis (1955), Hutchinson (1959) and Whitmore (1973) followed Engler (1897) in recognising Pentaphylacaceae as a monogeneric family. Anderberg et al. (Amer. J. Bot. 89 (2002) 677) place Pentaphylax and three genera of Ternstroemiaceae: Cleyera Thunb., Eurya Thunb. and Ternstroemia Mutis ex L.f. in a monophyletic group based on DNA sequence data. The trend now (Culham, Flow. Pl. Fam. World (2007) 248; Mabberley, Pl. Book, 3rd ed. (2008) 644) is to treat Pentaphylax as a distinct tribe within the enlarged Pentaphylacaceae, which includes two genera formerly grouped in Ternstroemiaceae. However, until molecular data is available for a wider range of genera, Pentaphylacaceae is treated here in the narrow sense with a single genus.
PENTAPHYLAX Gardner & Champ.
(Greek, penta = five, phyllassein = to guard; referring to five sepals protecting the flower bud)
J. Bot. & Kew Gard. Misc. 1 (1849) 244; Bentham, Fl. Hongkongensis (1861) 28; Bentham & Hooker f., Gen Pl. 1 (1862) 183; Ridley, Fl. Malay Pen. 1 (1922) 205; Gagnepain, Fl. Gén. Indo-China Suppl. 1 (1943) 332; Steenis, Fl. Malesiana 1, 5 (1955) 121; Whitmore, Tr. Fl. Malaya 2 (1973) 308; Weitzman et al., Fam. Gen. Vasc. Pl. 6 (2004) 456.
Shrubs or trees with dense crowns. Without stipules. Leaves simple, spirally arranged, pinnately veined. Inflorescences axillary, spike-like, often developing into foliate twigs, or with only solitary flowers along foliate twigs; bracteate, bracteoles 1–2, persistent. Flowers bisexual, actinomorphic, 5-merous; pedicel short or absent; sepals 5, imbricate; petals 5, imbricate, adnate with the stamens at the base; stamens 5, alternating with and shorter than petals, filaments broadest at the base, narrowing towards the anther, anther 2-locular, introrse, basifixed, opening by terminal pores; ovary superior, 5-locular, ovules 2 per locule, pendulous on axile placentas. Fruit a woody capsule, splitting loculicidally in the upper half or down to the base into 5 valves detaching from a persistent central column, each valve bearing a woody septum down the centre. Seeds laterally flattened, with a membranous wing at one end; embryo horseshoe-shaped, endosperm scarce.

Distribution. A genus with one widely distributed species, extending from SE China (including Hainan and Hong Kong, where it is a dominant species in montane evergreen forest), to Vietnam, N and C Sumatra, and Peninsular Malaysia.
Uses. It is sometimes cultivated as an ornamental.
Note. Pentaphylax is an aluminium accumulator and its leaves dry yellowish green due to their high aluminium content (Chenery, Kew Bull. (1948) 176).

Shrub or small to medium-sized tree, (2–)8(–18) m tall, beginning to flower at 60 cm, bole smooth, columnar, to 10 cm diameter, without buttresses. Bark dark brown to black, smooth or rugose, with lenticels, cracking and flaking-off in small papery scales; inner bark yellow to reddish brown, granular, brittle. Sapwood pale yellow to yellow and pink-tinged. Twigs black when dry, ridged lengthwise, sparsely minutely pubescent. Leaves in life with lateral and intercostal veins dark green contrasting pale yellowish green beneath, drying khaki, young leaves red; petioles slender, (1–)5(–10) mm long, grooved above, sparsely minutely pubescent or glabrous; laminas lanceolate to elliptic, rarely ovate or falcate, 3.3–5(–8.1) × 1.2–2.8 cm, chartaceous to thickly coriaceous, glossy above, sparsely minutely pubescent, subglabrous beneath, rarely sparsely, or minutely pubescent so near the base, base cuneate to slightly rounded, margin slightly revolute and sometimes minutely ciliate, apex acute, sometimes acuminate, tip minutely retuse, rarely minutely apiculate; midrib sparsely minutely pubescent, in the lower half grooved above, prominent beneath; lateral veins 7–12 pairs, fine, raised above, prominent beneath; intercostal veins finely reticulate, conspicuous, sometimes faint. Inflorescences (1–)2.3(–4) cm long; rachis ridged, minutely pubescent, rarely both inflorescences and solitary flowers on the same shoot; bracts and bracteoles deltoid, keeled, c. 1 mm long, minutely pubescent, margin ciliate. Flowers: buds light green to green, globular, to 2 mm diameter; pedicels to 1 mm, sepals deltoid, (0.5)–1 × c. 1 mm, unequal, outside minutely pubescent, inside keeled, glabrous, margin ciliate; petals white to pale green, sometimes tinged reddish, obovate to oblong, c. 2–3 × (0.5)–1.5 mm, glabrous, margin entire, apex rounded to slightly retuse; stamens 1–2 mm long, anthers c. 0.5 mm across,

pores opening by a minute beaked lid, attached at the lower end of the pore aperture; ovary ovoid, c. 0.5 mm long, 0.5 mm diameter, style cylindric, c. 1 mm long, stigma 5-lobed and star-shaped. Fruits green, sometimes with longitudinal light green lines, ripening brown to black, ellipsoid, rounded at both ends, (2–)4(–6) mm long, (4–)6(–8) mm diameter, glabrous, style persistent. Distribution. As for the genus; in Peninsular Malaysia recorded in Kedah, Perak, Kelantan, Pahang, Selangor, and Johor.
Conservation status. Least concern.
Ecology. In Peninsular Malaysia, it grows in lower to upper montane forest between 900–2000 m, mainly on rocky ridge tops. It flowers more-or-less continuously throughout the year. The dehiscent fruits with winged seeds suggest wind dispersal.
